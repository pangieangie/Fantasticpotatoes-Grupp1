THE NEXT-GENERATION TV SERIES RECOMMENDATION ALGORITHM

How our idea was born

When we started discussing what kind of dataset we wanted to work with, one thing became clear immediately: we needed a topic that actually sparks our interest. Something we use every day, something we care about, something that feels alive. Very quickly, someone mentioned movies. Another person added series. And suddenly a shared frustration unfolded: we all struggle to find a good series to watch.

Why TV series?

Even though we have endless streaming platforms, recommendation lists, and “Top 10” charts, it still feels surprisingly difficult to discover something that truly fits what we’re in the mood for. Sometimes the recommendations are too generic, sometimes they’re based on things we watched years ago, and sometimes they simply don’t understand what kind of story, tone, or emotional experience we’re looking for. We realized that this everyday problem is actually a perfect example of something that could be improved algorithmically.

So we decided to build a dataset around TV series and explore how an algorithm could help users find better matches based on more meaningful criteria. Instead of relying on broad categories like “Drama” or “Comedy,” we want to model our data in a way that captures deeper qualities: themes, emotional tone, pacing, complexity, representation, and maybe even the kind of experience the viewer wants (comfort, challenge, excitement, escape).

This led us into the conceptualisation phase:

  • Where do we find the data? We discussed using open databases like IMDb, but also realised we might need to create or curate parts of the dataset ourselves to capture the qualities we care about.
  • What format is the data in? Some sources provide CSV and some information might need to be added manually. We need to understand the structure of each source, which attributes exist, how series are described, and what is missing.
  • Do we need to clean or restructure the data? Yes. We need to remove irrelevant fields, standardise genres, add tags, or create new attributes that reflect the viewer’s experience more accurately.
  • How will the algorithm use the data? This is the core question. We need to anticipate what information the algorithm will need to make meaningful recommendations. That means modelling our dataset so that the algorithm can easily access the relevant attributes, for example, themes, mood, complexity, or viewer preferences.

Our Goal

In short, our conceptualization process began with a simple everyday frustration such as finding a good series. Then it grew into a project about designing a dataset that captures what really matters when choosing what to watch. Our goal is to create a structured, meaningful dataset that an algorithm can use to make smarter, more personalized recommendations than the ones we currently get.
